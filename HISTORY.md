# foodwise-india/  (aka "Jaano") — full history

Consumer-health app for understanding packaged foods sold in India — searchable food catalog, ingredient-concern decoder, side-by-side healthier-alternative swaps, corporate-parent directory, a daily food diary, and a **personal health/BMI engine**. **Live: https://kartikeyjaiswal42-sudo.github.io/foodwise-india/** (repo `kartikeyjaiswal42-sudo/foodwise-india`). Brand name in UI = **"jaano"** (title "Jaano — Know what you eat").
- **STACK = Next.js 15 App Router, static export (migrated from Vite 2026-06-11).** React 19 + `lucide-react`, no backend (localStorage). Layout `src/app/layout.js`, the whole SPA is one `'use client'` page `src/app/page.js`; components in `src/components/`, data in `src/data/`, pure logic in `src/lib/`. Global CSS = `src/app/globals.css` (font `@import` MUST stay first line). `next.config.mjs`: `output:'export'`, `images:{unoptimized:true}`, `trailingSlash:true`, and **`basePath:'/foodwise-india'` only when `PAGES_BASE=1`** (so a plain `next build` serves from root for local testing, deploy build adds the subpath). Run: `npm install` → `npm run dev`. **localStorage GOTCHA:** never read localStorage in a `useState` initializer — static prerender has no `window` and the export crashes; `page.js` hydrates log+profile in a mount `useEffect` gated by a `hydrated` flag. Components used only by the client page don't need their own `'use client'`.
- **v3.1 (2026-08-20) — SCRAPED TATA'S OWN SITES. THE HEADLINE FINDING IS A NEGATIVE ONE, AND IT WAS MEASURED.** User: *"scrape from tata website also"*. **Tata publishes NO nutrition panel anywhere.** tataconsumer.com, tetley.in, chingssecret.com and organicindia.com were all fetched and checked — marketing copy only (organicindia's Shopify `/products.json` mentions "rich in carbohydrates" in prose, which is not a panel). BigBasket is a **Tata Consumer company** and its product pages CAN carry a full declared panel — the ITC/Aashirvaad listing has energy/protein/carbs/total+added sugars/fibre/fat/satfat/sodium — so it was worth measuring rather than assuming: **0 of 56 Tata variants sampled across 8 brands had one**, and across the full run it settled at ~1%. Panels are supplied per-listing by the brand and **Tata has not supplied them**. So scraping cannot close the nutrition gap; that is a fact about Tata's data, not a limitation of the scraper, and the 374 unrated Tata products stay unrated.
- **WHAT THE SCRAPE IS ACTUALLY WORTH: the price field was a lie and now partly isn't.** `estPrice()` multiplies pack weight by a per-kilo rate guessed from the category — the UI has always labelled it "est. cost". BigBasket publishes real MRP, and **the measured gap is enormous**: Tata Sampann Sambar Masala 100 g estimated at **₹9, actual ₹85**; Fine Besan 1 kg estimated **₹9, actual ₹159**; Unpolished Urad Dal 1 kg estimated **₹90, actual ₹237**; Tata Salt Iodised 1 kg estimated **₹90, actual ₹28** (wrong in the other direction). 177 products now carry a printed MRP and the UI distinguishes them — `priceSource: 'bigbasket-mrp'` renders an **MRP** chip, everything else still says **est.** Conflating the two would have kept showing figures we now know to be wrong.
- **FOUR MATCHING BUGS, each one caught by eyeballing the matches before committing them — a wrong price written onto a real product is worse than the honest estimate it replaced.** (1) **A combo pack is not the product inside it** — "Pulses Combo Pack — Toor Dal & Chana Dal" ₹438 matched onto single Toor Dal. (2) **A price is only true for the pack it was printed on** — BigBasket's 2 kg listing at ₹398 was about to overwrite our 1 kg row; MRP now applies only when both sizes parse and agree within 5%, or (where our size was the placeholder "1 pack") size and price are adopted **together as a pair**, never the price alone. (3) **Duplicate tokens inflated the score above 1.0** — "Toor Dal/Arhar Dal" yields "dal" twice, so containment exceeded 1 and sailed past the 0.8 threshold on names that barely matched. (4) **A one-token name matches anything** — "Tata Salt" reduces to `["salt"]` once the brand word is dropped, so "Pink Salt For Everyday Cooking" scored a perfect 1.00 and would have written pink salt's ₹80 onto plain Tata Salt → **two shared tokens minimum**. Plus a `DISCRIMINATORS` list (whole/split/organic/unpolished/lite/pink/crusher/losodium/chilka…): if either name claims a discriminator the other does not, the match is refused, because "Masoor Dal Whole" and "Masoor Dal" are different SKUs. When several BigBasket SKUs land on one row the **cheapest wins** (₹32 plain black salt over the ₹130 crusher bottle) rather than iteration order deciding.
- **IMAGES ARE DELIBERATELY NOT TAKEN.** Product facts — name, weight, price — are data. BigBasket's product photography is copyrighted work, and hotlinking a commercial CDN from a public GitHub Pages site is neither licensed nor stable. The Open Food Facts images used elsewhere in this catalog are fine precisely because they are ODbL. A test asserts zero `bbassets`/`bigbasket` URLs reach the `image` field.
- **POLITENESS AND robots.txt, checked not assumed.** BigBasket disallows `/p/`, `/product/` and `/ps/` — none of which this touches; `/pb/` brand pages and `/pd/` product pages are permitted, and `/get_page_data/*` is explicitly Allowed (it needs a location id, so it is unused). tataconsumer.com and tetley.in disallow only `/core/`, `/profiles/`, `/admin/`, `/search/` and user paths. One request per 2.5 s, resumable via checkpoint so a retry costs one page rather than all of them. `scripts/out/` is gitignored.

- **TWO SCRAPER BUGS OF THE SAME FAMILY — a plausible number is the dangerous kind.** (1) **A per-SERVING panel read as per-100 g** understates a cereal by 3.3x and every individual figure still looks reasonable → `parseNutrition` parses the BASIS FIRST and anything not explicitly 100 g/ml is returned `per100:false` and dropped by the caller; it is never rescaled, because the serving size is itself often absent. (2) **`grab('Energy')` matched "Energy (kj): 1414" and stored kilojoules in the kcal field** — a 4.2x overstatement, caught by the new test suite → `grabEnergyKcal` requires the unit, and accepts a bare "Energy: 338" only when no kJ figure is present to contradict it. Also fixed: the ingredient cut pattern demanded an immediate colon after "Nutrition", so it skipped "Nutrition per gram/ml:" and cut at "Energy (kcal)" instead — leaving the panel's own header as the sole "ingredient".

- **v3.0 (2026-08-20) — MEAL PHOTOS, GHAR-vs-RESTAURANT, AND TATA 87 → 261.** Four features landed at once, so the navigation **shrank from 12 flat items to 6 sections with in-page tabs** (`SectionTabs.jsx`, `SECTIONS` in `page.js`) — the user's constraint was *"think of sync as everything will clutered is not likeable"*. **Every existing `navigate('<view>')` call site is untouched**: the view id stays the single source of truth and sections are a presentation layer over `VIEW_TO_SECTION`.
- **THE PHOTO ESTIMATOR (`MealCamera.jsx` + `lib/vision.js`) — the model IDENTIFIES, it never CALCULATES.** Gemini is handed OUR 223-dish list and constrained to pick from it; it answers only what a picture can support (which dish · how much · home/restaurant/dhaba/street, with the visual evidence it used). **Every calorie number is then computed by `lib/mealEstimate.js` from `data/indianDishes.js`** — so two users photographing the same dal get the same answer, the figure traces to a table, and the model cannot invent one. Same guardrail as bricktruth's "AIs extract and narrate, deterministic layer scores". `normalise()` DROPS any dish id not in our table (and reports it as `unmatched` rather than silently shortening the plate), clamps absurd quantities, and flags an inapplicable context as `contextKnown:false` so the band widens instead of hiding the guess.
- **KEY SAFETY — the whole reason it is BYO-key.** User: *"use only one when my gemini api is 100 safe as i am going to paste on linkedln"*. This is a public repo deploying to a public URL, so there is **nowhere in it a shared key could live**; a Worker proxy would have been the unsafe option (public URL + your key = strangers spending your quota). Each visitor's key lives only in their own `localStorage` and the image goes **browser → Google directly**. Backed by enforcement, not intention: **`scripts/check-no-secrets.mjs` runs in `prebuild` and `test`** and fails the build on a key-shaped string (Google/OpenAI/Anthropic/AWS/GitHub/Slack/private-key), with an ALLOW list for the placeholder and the format regex. **Proved able to fail** on a 39-char key in `.js`, a truncated key in `.json`, and a `.env.local`. Its first version was too brittle — anchored to exactly 39 chars with a trailing `\b`, so a 40-char paste slipped through; **a guard that only catches perfectly-shaped keys misses the one mangled by a bad copy-paste** → now `AIza[0-9A-Za-z_-]{30,}`.
- **THE HONESTY MODEL, inherited from `dataQuality.js`:** nobody can look at a bowl of dal and know its calories, so **`estimateMeal` refuses to emit a point value** — the UI renders `low–high` and every single figure is labelled a midpoint. Uncertainty compounds **in quadrature** from three independent sources (identity · portion · preparation): three ±20% errors give ±35%, not ±60%, and adding them linearly would make every estimate look uselessly vague. A consequence that is real maths rather than convenience: **a 4-item plate is proportionally MORE precise than one dish**, because over-estimating the dal partly cancels under-estimating the rice. A hand-corrected item is reclassified `confirmed`, so the range visibly tightens as you tell it more (photo ≈ ±30% → confirmed ≈ ±13% → built from measured ingredients < ±10%).
- **`data/indianDishes.js` — 223 dishes, home-vs-restaurant as a first-class dimension.** Per-serving values from IFCT 2017 against NIN household measures (katori 150 ml · roti ~40 g from 30 g atta · glass 250 ml · plate = one person). **Context ADDS FAT, it does not scale the dish** — restaurant dal has the same protein and fibre as ghar ki dal, it just carries butter and cream; a multiplier would imply the restaurant put more lentils in the bowl. Saturated share differs by context on purpose (restaurant 0.58 = butter+cream vs street 0.18 = refined frying oil) — collapsing them would make street food look like it hits blood lipids the way a cream gravy does. An unsupported context (there is no "street" home dal) **falls back to home and REPORTS the fallback** rather than quietly pricing a preparation nobody cooks. `aka` aliases make golgappa/puchka/gupchup all find pani puri — the same food has a different name in every state.
- **THE BUG A SCREENSHOT FOUND THAT 78 GREEN TESTS DID NOT: `CONTEXT_FAT` was declared per SERVING, which silently assumed every serving was a katori.** A 15 g tablespoon of achar was credited with a 150 g bowl's worth of restaurant oil and rendered **"+36 kcal, +86%" on a spoon of pickle**. The numbers were internally consistent — they were just about the wrong amount of food, which is exactly the class of error a unit test comparing the code to itself cannot see. Fix = the table is now **grams of added fat per 150 g of serving**, scaled in `servingFor` by the unit's real weight (cap 1.4× — a 250 g plate is not 1.7× as oily as a katori), with `ROTI` raised to 12/18/24 because butter sits on the surface of a 40 g bread rather than through a bowl. **The fix produced an independent cross-validation: plain naan + restaurant butter = 312 kcal, exactly the separately-written butter-naan row.** Also **drinks now get NO non-home model at all** — a restaurant does not add ghee to nimbu pani, and while a cafe chai probably does carry more milk and sugar, we have no figure for it, so no comparison is offered rather than a plausible-looking invented one.
- **`MealBuilder.jsx` + `data/indianIngredients.js` (91 raw ingredients, IFCT 2017).** The largest single error source in any Indian calorie estimate is the oil, and it is **invisible in a photograph** — tell the builder it was two teaspoons of ghee and that ±16% disappears. Also handles **serves**: a pot of dal is cooked once and eaten by four, and getting that division wrong is a 4× error. **THE TRAP THE TABLE HAD TO AVOID: 100 g of raw dal is not 100 g of cooked dal** (it roughly triples), so dry goods carry a `yield` and their "katori (cooked)" measure holds the RAW-equivalent weight — doing the conversion in both places is how a portion gets divided by three twice.
- **CROSS-TABLE VALIDATION, because two hand-built composition tables will drift:** an Atwater check (protein×4 + carbs×4 + fat×9 vs stated kcal) passes on **222 of 223** dishes — the one flag is green tea at 3 vs 2 kcal, pure rounding — and the two tables are asserted against each other: 30 g of atta from the ingredient table = 102 kcal, the chapati row = 104.
- **`Trends.jsx` + `lib/trends.js` — arithmetic only, and it stays quiet when it cannot speak.** Every sentence is a count over the user's own log; nothing infers a cause. **Minimum evidence is enforced, not suggested**: one logged day produces NO findings and says why, a trend claim needs 6 logged days, a home-vs-out comparison needs 2 servings each side. **An unlogged day is a hole in the data, not a zero-calorie day** — it is excluded from averages and drawn as a flat sliver the legend explains, because a skipped day rendered as a zero bar reads as a fast. `dayKey` uses LOCAL date, not `toISOString()`, which would file an 11 pm dinner in India under the previous day.
